Drones

How a drone's electronics fit together

A multicopter is a flying real-time system: a microcontroller closes control loops thousands of times per second, while a Linux computer next to it can see, plan and talk to the world. Here is a short, vendor-neutral tour of the building blocks I work with.

Typical architecture

🧠 Flight controller

What it does

  • Reads the IMU (gyros, accelerometers) at kHz rates, plus barometer, magnetometer and GNSS.
  • Fuses them in an estimator (an EKF) into attitude, velocity and position.
  • Runs cascaded control loops (rate → attitude → velocity → position) and mixes the output to the motors.
  • Owns safety: arming checks, geofence, battery and RC-loss failsafes.

The hardware

  • MCU: STM32F4/F7 in hobby boards; STM32H7 is today's standard for professional autopilots (Pixhawk 6-series class).
  • Sensors: often redundant, vibration-isolated and temperature-controlled IMUs.
  • Interfaces: DShot/PWM to ESCs, CAN (DroneCAN, Cyphal) for GNSS and ESCs, UART for telemetry and the companion computer.
  • Bootloader: lets new firmware be flashed over USB or CAN without a debugger.

🖥️ Companion computer

Why a second computer

The flight controller must stay small, deterministic and certifiable. Anything heavy runs next to it on Linux: cameras and video streaming, object detection, SLAM and obstacle avoidance, precision landing, mission logic and the long-range data link.

How they talk

  • MAVLink over UART or Ethernet, using MAVSDK, MAVROS or pymavlink.
  • uXRCE-DDS (PX4) to expose uORB topics directly as ROS 2 topics.
  • Typical boards: NVIDIA Jetson, Raspberry Pi, NXP i.MX 8M Plus.

📶 Links and communication

RC control

Low-latency pilot link: ExpressLRS/CRSF or SBUS receivers, with a failsafe if the link drops.

Telemetry

MAVLink over telemetry radios, mesh networks or IP links, to the ground station and between swarm members.

Beyond visual line of sight

LTE/5G and satellite links (e.g. Iridium Certus, Inmarsat) carry telemetry, commands and video far from the pilot.

🧪 Simulation and testing

SITL and HITL

Software-in-the-loop runs the real autopilot code against a simulator such as Gazebo; hardware-in-the-loop runs it on the actual flight controller. Most bugs should die here, not in the air.

Logs

Every flight leaves a log (PX4 ULog, ArduPilot DataFlash). Tools like PX4 Flight Review and UAV Log Viewer show vibration, estimator health and control tracking.

⚖️ Rules in the EU (short version)

  • Open category: low-risk flights within visual line of sight, at most 120 m above ground, split into subcategories A1/A2/A3 depending on the drone's class (C0–C4) and distance to people.
  • Specific category: higher-risk operations (e.g. BVLOS) need an operational authorisation, based on a risk assessment (SORA) or a standard scenario.
  • Certified category: manned-aviation-level risk, such as carrying people.
  • Operators register, pilots pass a competency test, and class-marked drones broadcast Remote ID.

A summary only; check EASA and your national aviation authority before flying.
